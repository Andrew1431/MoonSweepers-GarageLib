# Dune jumper (andrew1431.dune-jumper)

Lunar buggy line, tier 2. Stats: 1.08 m sweep strip, 16 m/s, 1900 kg hopper, $12,500.

## Pitch

The Lunar buggy's wild big brother: a long, high-riding trophy truck for the Moon. It sits on huge balloon tyres pushed
far out on exposed black A-arms and orange coilovers, with high white fibreglass fenders that leave room for the wheels
to travel. Its gimmick is the rear rocket-booster pod under the spare tyre, with a glowing orange nozzle for the jump,
plus a cyan light bar on the roof. It still reads as buggy line, with the same open roll cage, black bucket seats,
antenna with an orange tip and narrow orange roller up front, but it is longer (about 4.4 m), wider-tracked and carries a
proper hopper bed. The 1.08 m roller tucks right under the ramp nose.

## Parts

- Narrow white tub with orange side stripes, long ramp hood, black skid plate, black nose bumper, steel bumper hoop, headlights
- High white front fenders with orange stripes and black flare lips
- Two black bucket seats, dash, steering column and wheel (driver on the left, +X), small dark-glass windscreen
- Steel roll cage, white roof panel with orange stripe, black roof light bar with six cyan lamps
- White hopper bed (1.2 x 0.9 x 0.55 m) with orange lid, side stripes and orange tail lamps
- Rear rack braced off the cage, carrying the spare tyre (axle along Y) and the antenna
- Rocket-booster pod: white tube, black nose, orange band, four orange fins, black nozzle with an orange lamp glow
- Four 0.52 m radius chunky tyres with white hubs on a 2.0 m track and 2.35 m wheelbase
- Front double A-arms, uprights and orange coilovers poking through the hood; rear solid axle with diff, trailing arms, coilovers, mud flaps
- Front orange roller brush, exactly 1.08 m long, in a black hood on two short arms under the nose
- Root empty `DuneJumper_Root`

## Would improve

- The nozzle glow faces straight back, so it barely shows in the hero, side and front previews. A rear 3/4 preview would show it off, or the pod could be canted up a little.
- The front fenders are flat slabs. Real curved fenders, or a stepped profile, would look more like fibreglass.
- From the side, the front wheels hide most of the front suspension. Longer arms, or a lifted stance at full droop, would show off the long travel better.
- The budget is close to 20k because of the bevelled tread blocks (the spare is already slick). A lower tread count on the kit wheel would free up room for detail.
